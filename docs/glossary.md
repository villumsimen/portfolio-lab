# Glossary

Plain-language definitions. Terms are added as they appear in the project.

**Paper trading**: Simulating trades with fictional money, using real prices. No risk, and the same mistakes to learn from.

**Portfolio**: The collection of stocks you hold at a given time.

**NAV (net asset value)**: The total value of the portfolio right now: stocks plus cash. The line on the chart.

**Benchmark**: A reference to judge against. We ask "would I have done better just buying a global index fund?" MSCI World is the main one.

**MSCI World**: An index of about 1,400 large companies across developed countries. A fund tracking it is the "boring default" we try to beat.

**S&P 500**: An index of the 500 largest US companies.

**OSEBX**: The main index of the Oslo Stock Exchange (Norway).

**Quality (factor)**: How good the business itself is: profitable, financially solid, steady. Measured with the metrics below.

**Value (factor)**: How cheap the company looks relative to what it earns. A cheap price for a good business is the idea.

**QARP (quality at a reasonable price)**: Buy good businesses, but not at any price.

**Sector**: The industry a company is in (banks, tech, energy...). We compare stocks within their sector, since a bank and a software firm aren't comparable.

**Z-score**: How far a number is from the average of its group, in standard steps. A z-score of +1 means "clearly above average", 0 means average, and -1 clearly below.

**Look-ahead bias**: Accidentally using information in a test that wasn't public yet at the time. It makes backtests look better than reality.

**Backtest**: Replaying a strategy on past data to see how it would have done. Useful for learning, but never proof.

**Survivorship bias**: Testing only on companies that still exist today, ignoring those that failed. It makes results look too good.

**Total return**: Price change plus dividends. The honest number.

**Dividend**: A cash payment a company makes to its shareholders.

**Slippage**: The small difference between the price you expected and the price you actually get when trading.

**FX (foreign exchange)**: Currency exchange rates. Owning a US stock means owning dollars too, so a falling dollar lowers your kroner value.

**Hedging**: Paying to cancel out a risk (here: currency). We don't hedge at first.
